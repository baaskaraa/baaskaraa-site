Biography

 

Short biography

baaskaraa (b. 1974, Tamil Nadu, India) is an Indian artist based in Singapore, working across painting, sculpture, digital art and Tamil poetry. Trained at the Government College of Arts and Crafts, Chennai, India's oldest art school, he entered the collection of the National Gallery of Modern Art, New Delhi, while still a student. He is a two-time Golden Point Award winner for Tamil Poetry and a PromaxBDA Global Excellence Award winner.

Biography

baaskaraa (b. 1974, Tamil Nadu, India) is an Indian artist based in Singapore, working across painting, sculpture, digital art and Tamil poetry. What sets him apart is literature: he is an artist who writes, a prize-winning Tamil poet and short story writer who knows literature from the inside.

He trained at the Government College of Arts and Crafts, Chennai, India's oldest art school, graduating with First Class Distinction. While still a student, his work was shown at the Lalit Kala Akademi's 40th National Exhibition of Art and acquired by the National Gallery of Modern Art, New Delhi; in 1998 he received the Victoria Technical Institute's Mahatma Gandhi Birth Centenary Memorial Award and the Tamil Nadu State Award.

In Singapore since 2000, he was twice honoured with the Golden Point Award for Tamil Poetry, and served the Association of Singapore Tamil Writers for nine years, working beside the pioneers of Singapore Tamil literature and helping organise the first World Tamil Writers Conference in 2011. His installation Bird Sanctum (2014), after a Tamil novel, was commissioned by the National Arts Council and the National Library Board. His design work for CNA, Mediacorp, earned a PromaxBDA Global Excellence Award in Los Angeles.

Biography · long

The full story

I · The village · 1974–1994

 

He was born Baskar in 1974 in Irumbulikkurichi, a village in the Ariyalur district of Tamil Nadu, India.

The first images he knew were not in books. In the village stands the shrine of his family's deity, the kula deivam: an open-air sanctuary of giant painted horses and elephants, seated guardian warriors and small domed shrines beneath a great tree. Figures larger than a man, painted in full colour, standing in the open.

At school he won first prizes three years running: for drawing in 1988 and 1989 at the Government High School, Irumbulikkurichi, and for painting in 1990 at the Government Higher Secondary School, Ponparappi. That same year he took second prize for an essay. The writer was already there.

In 1992 he passed the Government Technical Examination, Higher Grade, in freehand outline and model drawing. Two years later he left for Chennai and its art school.

The village never left the work. His home there is one of his three studios, and since 2014 Aalam has joined the village's people working in Singapore to the village at home.

II · Chennai · 1994–2000

 

In 1994 he entered the Government College of Arts and Crafts, Chennai. Founded in 1850, it is India's oldest art school. The preparatory course took him through drawing, painting, printmaking, visual communication design, textile design, ceramics, photography and sculpture.

In his first year a watercolour of white daisies sold through the Victoria Technical Institute. It was his first sale, and it gave him the confidence to go on.

By 1997 he was exhibiting across India. With the Square Group, four young artists at Saga Art Gallery, Chennai, he drew reviews in The Hindu and Dinamani. His work was shown at the 40th National Exhibition of Art in Bangalore, and that year a work of his entered the collection of the National Gallery of Modern Art, New Delhi. A college study trip to Ooty set off the Ooty series: far landscapes, wholly imagined.

1998 brought three honours. The Victoria Technical Institute's Mahatma Gandhi Birth Centenary Memorial Award, for the oil painting My Native Land. The State Award of the Tamil Nadu Oviya Nunkalai Kuzhu, for Light on Scape Series-1, which The Hindu singled out for its handling of light. Second prize at Gandhi Mandapam, presented in the presence of the Governor and the Chief Minister of Tamil Nadu.

In 1999 he graduated Bachelor of Fine Arts in Painting, First Class with Distinction, with the Best Outgoing Student Award. The same year he earned a Diploma in Multimedia Design and Presentation, Grade A: the screen was already calling. He began the Master of Fine Arts, and in May 2000 held his first solo exhibition, A Portfolio of Art Happenings, at Apparao Galleries, Chennai. In October he left for Singapore.

III · The painter who fell into language · 2000–2013

 

In 2000 he landed in Singapore with a painter's hands and no canvas. What he found instead was a language: his own.

For nearly a decade he worked inside Tamil, building it in pixels and sound for the children of a new country. He drew it, animated it and edited its voice, frame by frame and syllable by syllable. Day after day the language passed through his hands, and something turned. His thoughts stopped arriving as images. They arrived as words.

So he wrote.

In 2003 he married Suguna, a poet. Two writers at one table, then twice a month at Kavimaalai and Kavi Solai, the Tamil poetry circles where Singapore's poets read and are judged by each other. It went on for over a decade. The circles became the national stage: the Golden Point Award for Tamil Poetry in 2011, and again in 2013, when Suguna took first prize in the same ceremony. Two poets, one home, one language.

In 2004 he became a life member of the Association of Singapore Tamil Writers. In 2006 his short story அம்மா தந்தது (Amma Thanthathu, "What Mother Gave") won an Incentive Prize, presented by Tharman Shanmugaratnam, then Minister for Education, at the ASTW's 30th anniversary Muthamizh Vizha. He served on its committee from 2007 to 2015, and in 2011 sat on the organising committee of the World Tamil Writers Conference, Singapore.

IV · From the word, the form · 2007–2014

 

Then the words came back to the canvas. Literature stopped only inspiring the work and started entering it directly.

Bird Sanctum was the first time the public saw it: a Tamil novel turned into nests of real twigs, touring Singapore's national libraries in 2014. Commissioned for Project LAVA by the National Arts Council and the National Library Board, it grew from Ma. Elangkannan's novel Kuruvi Kottam. The twigs for the nest were gathered at MacRitchie Reservoir with the novelist himself. The work travelled to library@chinatown, Ang Mo Kio, Bedok and Jurong East, and on 18 July artist and novelist spoke together at Ang Mo Kio Library.

The same year he gave form to a poem by Suguna Baskar, Sinegam Theerndha Kaadhal: first as a digital sculpture, then as a painting.

The work also left the studio. In 2013, for Maya Dance Theatre's production GREY, he and the photographer Charlie Lim made the murals on the rooftop of the Singai Tamil Sangam in Little India, guiding the dancers, residents and students who painted them, with thanks to the National Arts Council. For four nights in June the walls were the stage. The murals have since been painted over; the photographs remain.

The theatre had called earlier. In 2007 his digital backdrops framed 37 Pearls at the Victoria Theatre, an evening of Tamil theatre and dance by the Singapore Indian Artistes' Association and the Manimaran Dance Group.

He doesn't illustrate literature. He writes it, and then he builds it.

V · The same hand, on screen · 2001–2023

 

Alongside the poet, a second discipline was growing: the screen. It began in 2001 with digital creation for Tamil language and culture, for Ministry of Education projects, and it became a profession.

From November 2008 he was Art and Visual Effects Director for television at Tantra Inc., Singapore: opening titles, trailers and visual effects for television series, art direction and effects for commercials, set design.

From 2010 to 2014 he worked independently, as a full-time painter, digital freelancer and founder of the RiGHTMiND studio.

In 2014 he joined Mediacorp as Senior Graphic Designer at CNA, Singapore's international news channel, and stayed nine years, designing motion graphics and branding for the channel. A CNA logo animation is his, from concept to final frame.

In 2015 that work was recognised in Los Angeles: a PromaxBDA Global Excellence Award, Bronze, in Art Direction & Design, Program-Specific Logo, for CNA's Documentary of the Week, presented at PromaxBDA's annual global conference alongside winners such as CNN and FX. In 2021 Mediacorp honoured him with its Long Service Award.

The screen never replaced the canvas. It trained the same hand in time, motion and light: the hand that now builds digital sculpture, 3D works and virtual space.

VI · Agam. Puram. · since 2010

 

In 2010 a second journey began, inward. Agam, the inner path, led him through the yogis and siddhas of Tamil tradition, to Arunachala and the temples of Tamil Nadu, back to the village gods, and to sit with masters in India and Singapore. Vasi Yoga in the Siddha tradition. Hatha Yoga from 2016. Naadham நாதம் from 2018.

Stillness opened into pure observation: a dialogue with no intention, in which questions arose and answered themselves. In his words: "Stillness opens into observation. A dialogue begins without intention. Questions arise and answer themselves. What remains is the artwork." What remained, in May 2020, was Emergence: a living plant traced as line, the line set free, the line resolved into a gold sculpture in three dimensions.

Puram, the outer path, runs the other way. On 14 January 2014 he brought together the people of Irumbulikkurichi working in Singapore as Aalam, for the village at home: Pongal, village festivals, honouring the elders.

The line itself is the signature: non-stop, never defined, everything connected and flowing. The Unbroken Line, the line studies and a self-portrait, all of 2020, carry it.

Now the work turns on literature again. Vinayagar Agaval, after Avvaiyar's verse, has been in the making since a sketch of 3 February 2020. Kavi, a study of Suguna Baskar in gouache and Japanese gold, opens the way to a planned life-size portrait in oil and 24-carat gold.

Projects

The work beyond the studio

2014 · NATIONAL ARTS
COUNCIL AND NATIONAL
LIBRARY BOARD

Bird Sanctum · Project LAVA

Bird Sanctum was one of four artworks chosen for the second Project LAVA in the Libraries, the NAC and NLB programme that pairs Singapore writing with visual art. It turned Ma. Elangkannan's Tamil novel Kuruvi Kottam into an installation of white birds around a nest that stands for home, on a pedestal in the shape of Singapore, and it travelled to four public libraries from May to August 2014.

2013 · SINGAI TAMIL
SANGAM ROOFTOP,
LITTLE INDIA

GREY · Maya Dance Theatre

GREY was a Maya Dance Theatre production staged on the rooftop of Singai Tamil Sangam, 02 Kampong Kapor Road, Little India, from 19 to 22 June 2013 at 8 pm. The programme credits the visuals and murals to Charlie Lim and Ka. Baskar: the rooftop walls, stairhouse and parapets were painted as a mural that became the environment for the dance, with members of the Kampong Glam Rowell Court Residents' Committee and students from the Republic Polytechnic Indian Cultural Group painting alongside. Baskar also made a promotional video in support of the event. With thanks to the National Arts Council.

2007 · SIAA AND
MANIMARAN DANCE
GROUP

37 Pearls · Victoria Theatre

முத்துக்கள் 37 PEARLS was an evening of Tamil theatre and dance at Victoria Theatre on Saturday 3 March 2007, presented jointly by the Singapore Indian Artistes' Association and the Manimaran Dance Group, with Dr SP Thinnappan as Guest of Honour. Baskar created the background scenery: digital stage backdrops for the programme, which included the historical play Raja Raja Chozhan, dances by the Manimaran group and the comedy 37-ம் புலிகேசி.
